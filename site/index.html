Reading AI-Written Code

A non-coder's guide to using AI-written code safely

A plain-English manual for people who get code written for them by AI helpers: what code even does, how to read it without being a programmer, spotting the "trust me" moments, asking what could break, testing in a playground before you rely on anything, keeping originals safe, and the copy-run-rollback routine that ends with a tool you actually own. Includes an annotated walkthrough of a real, runnable example script — plus a printable safety checklist and a pocket glossary.

Once you have this one analogy, 90% of what confuses beginners about "code" stops being mysterious, and the safety chapters in this book start to feel obvious rather than exotic.

  • Chapter 4 — Asking What Could Break

    Chapter 3 trained your ear for over-confidence. This chapter gives you the questions — a playbook of asks that, used before running anything, will catch most of what can go wrong. It's structured as a conversation you can have with your AI in a few minutes. The questions are also useful, with light adaptation, for a friend or nephew who wrote you a script: the goal is precise communication about risk, not interrogation.

    Chapter 4 · ~8 min read

  • Chapter 5 — Testing Before You Rely on It

    You've read the code (Chapter 2), heard the trust-me claims (Chapter 3), and gotten the risk list (Chapter 4). Now comes the step that actually earns your confidence: running the thing — carefully. This chapter is the linker between "code that exists" and "code I can rely on," and it's built around one sentence: test in a classroom, not the kitchen. You never want a first-ever run aimed at files you care about. Practice on the copies and the fakes; build confidence cheaply; then aim it at the real thing.

    Chapter 5 · ~7 min read

  • Chapter 6 — Keeping Originals Safe

    Everything in Chapters 1–5 only works if one thing is guaranteed underneath: when a run goes wrong, the originals survive. This chapter is about making that guarantee boring, cheap, and repeatable. It's written for people whose data includes irreplaceables — years of photos, a manuscript only they wrote, their business's records — and who have deleted something once in their life and never want to feel that again.

    Chapter 6 · ~6 min read

  • Chapter 7 — Secrets and Paths to Check

    Two things cause the real damage when AI-written code goes wrong: secrets (credentials that shouldn't be seen, shared, or uploaded) and paths (places in your file system the script shouldn't go). Neither one is exotic. Both show up in tiny beginner scripts. Both are checkable in a couple of minutes once you know what to look for. This chapter gives you the two scans that, between them, cover the worst practical outcomes of running code you didn't write.

    Chapter 7 · ~7 min read

  • Chapter 8 — The Copy-Run-Rollback Routine

    Everything so far — reading, distrusting, questioning, testing, snapshots, secret-and-path scans — now folds into one repeatable loop you'll run on every piece of AI code you take seriously. It's called copy-run-rollback, and once it's a habit it will take you about ten minutes, start to finish (fifteen the first few times — that's honest and normal). It's the routine this whole book exists to teach, and by far the most important muscle here.
